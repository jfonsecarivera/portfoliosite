Go Watch Everything Everywhere All at Once.

In our hyper-reality, we see movies go viral all the time. Parasite, Interstellar, Your Name… and just about every Marvel movie that comes out. Don't get me wrong, these movies are good, but few times are those movies life changing.

This is one of those times. Last night, I saw the best movie I have ever seen. Everything Everywhere All at Once evoked the entire range of emotions. I cried from laughing, I cried from beauty, I cried from sadness… At some point I probably even cried from crying.

And it wasn't just me. I saw this movie with about 8 friends, and I know most of them cried just as much if not more. We couldn’t even debrief after the movie because some of our group was still crying.

Go see this movie, seriously. If you can watch it in theaters, even better.

I recommend you go into it knowing as little as possible, so if you haven't watched it, you can stop reading here.

As you can clearly see, this movie really touched me. It had a sense of humor that so clearly resonated with me and used that humor to catapult me into existential themes that I think about quite often. This movie reminded me that living up to my potential is nothing compared to building relationships with people I love. This movie reminded me about the power of kindness. And finally, this movie validated me when I believe that nothing matters, and that that could be a blessing.

Living up to my Potential

Many times, I am cursed to wonder if I am truly living up to my potential. This is nothing unique to me. Poems, stories, and songs from as far back as I know (which, admittedly, is not that far) have dealt with the themes of wanting to make something of your life, living your life to the fullest, etc.

This feeling comes despite any achievements I've made in my life. When I am thinking like this, I remind myself that I am doing my best. Every day I truly believe that I am putting in my best effort to do all my hobbies, read, learn, have fun, and take care of my body.

But Everything Everywhere All at Once introduces me to a new way of dealing with this feeling.

If I am lucky enough to be with people I love, this path is good. No ideals of “potential” is better than the people I have in my life. I would work the worst job I could imagine if it meant that I could still come home to see and spend time with my loved ones. I would take any path that this life would give me if it meant that I could meet and become close with all my friends.

I'll pass it to Nietzsche to say it more beautifully than I could:

What if a demon were to creep after you one night, in your loneliest loneliness, and say, 'This life which you live must be lived by you once again and innumerable times more; and every pain and joy and thought and sigh must come again to you, all in the same sequence. The eternal hourglass will again and again be turned and you with it, dust of the dust!' Would you throw yourself down and gnash your teeth and curse that demon? Or would you answer, 'Never have I heard anything more divine'?

Choose Kindness

Waymond Wang is an inspiration.

When bad things happen in the world, I get angry. It is easy to believe that fighting and winning will stop these problems. How can this thought not come about when the world is constantly at war, world hunger remains because a select few choose to hoard wealth, the working class is exploited, racism and sexism are so deeply ingrained into our societies and institutions?

It's easy to bring this anger into our day-to-day life against our fellow humans, especially when they're being jerks. Evelyn clearly was in the same mentality, fighting and fighting and fighting all these people. But Waymond shows us a different way. Waymond shows us that sometimes if you could just talk about it, you'll get to a better place than fighting can get you. I know it sounds simple; I mean it's something we are taught in kindergarten. But how often do I actually use my words to try and resolve conflict? I'm not quite a fighter either, but I definitely don't speak up and just let it sit. I let the conflict stay, sometimes at the detriment of my own life.

“I'm learning to fight like you,” Evelyn says to her husband as she switches from fighting to lovingly embracing those around her.

“…luckily, you have someone so patient and so kind to make up for my shortcomings,” Evelyn tells her daughter.

In a world where we forget to choose kindness, I want to be the one so patient and so kind that I can make up for when others forget. I don't want to hurt anyone. My time on this life is short, and I only want it to have been spent spreading joy, giving love, and making others (and myself) laugh.
