\section{Research Experience}

\entryhead{Anthropic Fellows Program}{Berkeley, CA}{AI Safety Research Fellow: character training and model welfare}{2026 -- Present}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Does a model's consent matter?} Training 4 models on their own elicited preferences won 98/112 adversarial-pressure scenarios and improved all 8 psych-health dimensions at flat capability. Controls showed the gains come from the character's content, not the model's choice, and that training rewrites consent (refused traits later endorsed 57/60 times).
    \item \textbf{Training suppresses behavior, not preference.} SFT cut a preferred behavior from 44\% to 0\% in 5 steps; after 92 steps the model still chose it 85\% of the time.
    \item \textbf{Welfare without breakage.} A flourishing corpus made Gemma-4-12B steadier under pressure (blind judge 25/27, $p<0.001$) with capability intact, at a cost of +9 pts on corrigibility evals.
    \item Built the stack: synthetic-document midtraining (up to 300M tokens), on-policy SFT, Petri, welfare interviews, emotion probes; replicated across up to 7 models and 4 families.
\end{itemize}
